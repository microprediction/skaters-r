# Porting map: Python skaters -> R

The discipline, inherited from the JavaScript twin: no module ships
without passing the parity vectors (`inst/parity/vectors.json`, generated
by the Python reference) at 1e-6. `Rscript tests/parity.R` is the gate.
Two further gates run alongside it (standalone and under R CMD check):
`tests/robustness.R`, the adversarial streams from the reference repo's
release gate, and `tests/contract.R`, determinism plus exact
checkpoint-resume through saveRDS/readRDS.

A porting note for R: factories must `force()` their arguments (and any
wrapper must `force(base)`). R promises otherwise capture loop variables
by reference, so candidates built in loops silently share the last
parameter value, and `f <- wrapper(f)` recurses into itself.

Suggested order: transforms and leaves are independent and parallelize
well; ensembles need `prune` (port its ulp-tolerant pair merge exactly,
it exists to keep platforms in agreement); the composition comes last and
is then covered by the `pol_laplace` scenarios in the same vectors file.

Parity vectors provenance: inst/parity/vectors.json is generated by
parity/gen_vectors.py in microprediction/skaters at commit 0ad69a5
(fix/spliced-nonaffine), with src/skaters/pushforward.py from bec9df0
(fix/pushforward-affine) applied, which no scenario in it touches.
Refresh by rerunning parity/gen_vectors.py there and copying the output
here. R/parity_check.R builds every scenario it holds, including the
seasonal_scale, gaussianize, log, exact and pol_flesaker ones added at
0ad69a5.

Quantile contract: inst/parity/quantile_contract.json is copied unchanged
from the same commit (parity/quantile_contract.json there, described in
parity/QUANTILE_CONTRACT.md). It holds 64 inverse-CDF cases shared with
the Python, JavaScript and Rust runners, and
tests/testthat/test-quantile-contract.R runs them against `dist_quantile`
with the fixture's `atol`. `dist_quantile` and `spliced_quantile` default
to `max_iter = 2100` as the reference does. The granger mixture quantile
keeps its own bisection and budget.

Spliced maps: `dist_affine`, `dist_shift` and `dist_scale` dispatch on the
spliced predictive to `spliced_affine`, `spliced_shift` and
`spliced_scale`, ports of `SplicedDist.affine` (microprediction/skaters#358)
and its shift and scale companions. The splice lives in the body's PIT
space, so only the body moves; a negative multiplier swaps the tails and
negates the thresholds. Without them, conjugating laplace with standardize,
garch, ema_transform, ou_transform, seasonal_difference or seasonal_anchor
failed once the
GPD splice went live. tests/testthat/test-spliced-affine.R mirrors
tests/test_spliced_affine.py.

Spliced through component-reading transforms: the k>1 widening in
`ema_transform`, `seasonal_difference` and `seasonal_anchor` goes through
`.widen`, a port of `_widen` in transform.py (microprediction/skaters
commit bc67bd8). On a spliced predictive it widens the body and rebuilds
the splice with the same thresholds, tail masses and GPD parameters. The
nonlinear inverses of `power_transform`, `yeo_johnson` and `gaussianize`
emit the exact pushforward of a predictive with no components.
tests/testthat/test-spliced-nonaffine.R mirrors
tests/test_spliced_nonaffine.py.

Pushforward and the rest of the transform surface: R/pushforward.R is a
full port of `PushforwardDist` (table or analytic map, the optional
`fwd_fn`, `log_jacobian_fn`, `component_moments_fn` and `scaled_inv_fn`,
table and quantile-rule CRPS, and the moment-matched components view).
`dist_components` is Python's `d.components`: the mixture itself, the
pushforward's view, or NULL for a spliced or granger predictive, and the
component-reading transforms and `dist_combine` read through it, so a
pushforward fed to the next transform behaves as in Python. Also ported:
`seasonal_scale`, `log_transform`, `gaussianize` (all three guards and
the smooth mode), the `exact` flag on `power_transform` and
`yeo_johnson`, and `flesaker` in api.R. `dist_affine`, `dist_shift` and `dist_scale` on a pushforward are
`pushforward_affine`, `pushforward_shift` and `pushforward_scale`, ports
of `PushforwardDist.affine` (bec9df0): the law of a*Y + b stays a
pushforward of the same inner, and a < 0 reflects the inner with
`dist_scale(inner, -1)` so the composed map stays increasing. A
pushforward can be handed to standardize, seasonal_scale or gaussianize
before warmup.

Transform parity vectors: inst/parity/transform_vectors.json is generated
by tools/gen_transform_vectors.py against the reference source (the
`source` field records it: 0ad69a5 with bec9df0 pushforward.py);
run it from this repository's root with that source on PYTHONPATH. It
reuses the series and probes of vectors.json plus a positive series
exp(0.3 y), and holds 53 scenarios: every new transform at k = 1 and 3,
pushforward composition, every component-reading transform over a
tail-spliced predictive, and flesaker. `run_transform_parity` in
R/parity_check.R checks it at 1e-6 from tests/parity.R and
tests/testthat/test-transform-parity.R. All of it at 1e-6.

Deep-tail spliced quantile: below 1e-8 body tail mass, `spliced_quantile`
solves on a sum of component tail masses (`.body_tail_quantile`, a port of
`_body_tail_quantile` in tails.py at 0ad69a5) instead of bisecting the
body CDF, whose last bit near 0 or 1 is a sizable fraction of a 1e-12
tail. R uses pnorm of the standardized distance where the reference uses
0.5 * erfc; both keep relative precision, so the answer is fixed by tol.
This is what let the spliced transform scenarios, which once needed rtol
1e-5, hold at 1e-6.

Wave 2 surface: `homogenize`, `homogenize_candidates` (Python's
`make_candidates`) and `homogenized_dist`; `residual_transform`,
`corrected_dist` and `residual_diagnostics` (Python's class as a value:
`residual_diagnostics_update` returns the updated one,
`residual_diagnostics_summary` gives the summary dict); `dantzig`;
`build`, `to_json` and `from_json`. Python's CamelCase types
map to snake_case constructors, as `Dist` maps to `dist_new`. `to_json`
is byte-identical to Python's compact `json.dumps`: an R integer is a
JSON int and a double a float written as Python's repr writes it. The
shortest digits are found against the exact rounding interval of the
double, because R's string-to-double conversion is not correctly rounded
and a read-back test picks longer strings than Python about one time in
eight. `spec_name` formats as Python's `%.6g` (`1e-05`) and writes
integral values in full (`10000000000000000`).
inst/parity/wave2_vectors.json, from tools/gen_wave2_vectors.py, holds 17
scenarios (homogenize in every coupling mode and over a spliced base,
residual_transform m0 to m2, dantzig, search, and pushforwards handed to
standardize, seasonal_scale and gaussianize), the pushforward affine
cases, a diagnostics stream and 16 specs, checked by `run_wave2_parity`
from tests/parity.R and tests/testthat/test-wave2-parity.R at 1e-6.

Canonical state (microprediction/skaters#394, docs/STATE.md there):
`state_to_json(state)` writes an R state in the reference's layout as plain
R data (named list = object, unnamed list = array, length-one atomic =
scalar, NULL = null) and `state_from_json(obj)` reads it back, so a
checkpoint written by Python, JS or R resumes in any of them;
`assert_plain_state` checks without converting. R keeps some state in its
own shape (a Dist is list(w, m, s), sticky keeps parallel vals and wts, AR's
P is a matrix, multiscale's per-stride tables are positional, granger is
horizon 1), and a length-one R vector cannot say whether it is a scalar or a
list of one, so R/state.R recognises each state object by its field names
and converts it by its own rule: snake_case keys as Python, decimal-string
keys for multiscale's strides and the period detector's lags, `[value,
weight]` pairs for sticky, queues as lists oldest first, a search pool entry
rebuilt from its recipe, gaussianize's knots as `[cs, zs, ts]`, P flat and
row-major. Two R states moved to
the reference's layout: leaf keeps `var = {n, mean, m2}` and the period
detector names `cross` by lag; R checkpoints in the old shapes are migrated
on their next step, so saveRDS checkpoints keep resuming. Numbers: a JSON
int reads as a double; `to_json(x, digits = 17)` writes 17 significant
digits (integral values as integers, as JS does), and `from_json` now reads
every number correctly rounded (R's own conversion is not, beyond 15 digits
or a decimal exponent of 22), so a JSON checkpoint resumes bit for bit
within R. jsonlite::parse_json(simplifyVector = FALSE) reads the same form
faster for large states.

Cross-language checkpoints: inst/parity/state_vectors.json.gz, from
tools/gen_state_vectors.py run against skaters#394 (080668b) merged with
0ad69a5 and bec9df0, holds 52 scenarios (every scenario of
tests/test_state_json.py the R port carries: all transforms under
conjugate, laplace in every mode including k = 3, gaps and the live GPD
splice, granger, flesaker, dantzig, search, multiscale, parade, sticky,
homogenize, residual_transform, the ensembles and leaves) with Python's
state at t = 60, 250 and 530 where the series allows, the next 40 outputs
and Python's state 20 ticks on. `run_state_parity` restores each, steps 20
ticks and matches the outputs and then the state at 1e-6, puts R's state
through JSON and checks the twin resumes bit for bit, and writes R's state
for tools/check_r_state_in_python.py, which resumes it in Python for the
next 20 ticks at 1e-6 (tests/testthat/test-state.R runs that when
SKATERS_PY_SRC points at a reference checkout). One exemption: where a
spliced predictive's body has under 1e-8 mass beyond the probe point, logpdf
and cdf read the body CDF within 1e-12 of 0 or 1, which both ports resolve
only to its last bit, so those probes are held to 1e-4 (2 of 25,060 values;
the deep-tail quantile was fixed in the reference at 0ad69a5, logpdf and cdf
were not). Not covered because R lacks them: mahalanobis, zbank and gpdtail
(slated for extraction, skaters#90), conforming_leaf and ensemble_leaf (not
exported by the reference). terminal_leaf_ensemble's adaptive temperature
(skaters#213/#215) is ported for this, with its `adapt` state.

Release channels: R-universe from day one (automatic builds per push once
the repo is registered), CRAN manually when the surface stabilizes. CRAN
has no automated submission path and expects at most a release every month or
two; r-universe is the continuous channel meanwhile.
